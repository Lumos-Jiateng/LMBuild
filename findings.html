Analysis

Findings

Frontier models build convincing outer shells. What they lack is the internal mechanism that makes an object work.

01

Closed APIs lead on every level

GPT-6 Astra scores 88.5 / 50.0 / 74.0 / 53.5 on Soundness / Affordance / Design / Realization, with Claude Opus 5 and Claude Fable 5.1 close behind. The strongest open model, Qwen3.5-27B, comes close on Soundness (82.9) and matches them on Realization (57.2), but trails by about 20 points on Affordance and Design.

02

Function, not physics, is the bottleneck

A.3 kinematics (about 38) and R.3 operability (about 18) are the lowest scores of the frontier models. 57% of failed motion targets fail because the moving part is never built.

03

Building less looks good on single metrics

Small assemblies (a median of 8 parts for Qwen3.5-27B against 20 for GPT-6 Astra) score higher on connectivity, assembly order and materials, and far lower on parts, kinematics and fidelity.

04

3D generators win on form, not function

Brick generators are collision-free by construction, and Cube3D produces the most polished shapes (D.2 = 82.2), but their fidelity, parts and kinematics remain far below the agents'.

05

Product CAD is the hardest source

On complete open-source hardware products, 81% of frontier designs miss or misplace required components, against 48% on Artiverse furniture and appliances.

06

The metrics are stable

Two disjoint 50-object samples rank the 30 systems the same way on every metric (Pearson r 0.937 to 0.988), so the 200-object leaderboard is a robust estimate.

Interpretation

How to read single metrics

On a few metrics another system matches or exceeds GPT-6 Astra. Each metric isolates one property, and several properties can be met by construction, by building less, or by optimising appearance rather than function. None of these cases makes the system a better builder.

Soundness of brick generators

BrickGPT reaches S.1 = 99.6 and S.2 = 100 because it builds on a stud lattice and rejects any colliding brick. Its Design score is 24.2: the structure holds together but rarely resembles the requested object.

Soundness of segmented meshes

Particulate splits one generated mesh into parts, so the parts tile a surface and cannot interpenetrate (S.2 up to 96.5). The same parts are not designed to bear load: PartCrafter's stability is 11 to 14.

Simpler designs on per-design rates

S.1, R.1 and R.2 are rates that a small assembly satisfies more easily. GPT-6 Astra itself reaches R.1 = 0.74 on designs with at most 10 parts and 0.56 above 20. Gaps and materials are cheap to fix; missing mechanisms are not.

Aesthetics without identity

D.2 is judged without a reference, so that it does not reward copying the photo. A text-only generator can score high with a polished generic shape (a monitor on a stand for "monitor arm"). Read D.2 together with D.3, where Cube3D scores 57.7 against 74.9 for GPT-6 Astra.

Named parts from the input

CubePart takes a part schema as input, which in our protocol is the task's component list, so its parts carry the expected names. Its A.2 of 32.7 partly reflects that input; none of its capabilities moves in simulation.

Materials of PhysX-Anything

The only generator that predicts materials scores R.2 = 61.4, but its parts are coarse (A.2 = 8.7) and nothing it builds operates (R.3 = 1.9).

Error analysis

Where frontier models fail

Share of the 200 tasks on which each closed-source API shows a failure mode. Every model submits a design on every task.

Toaster · Claude Opus 5

Builds the bread slot but none of the lever, timer, thermal sensor or ejection mechanism, so none of its five capabilities works.

Toilet · Claude Opus 5

A correct seat hinge, but no lid or flush handle, and none of the internal trap, drain pipe or flush valve.

Off-road vehicle · GPT-6 Astra

Wheels rotate correctly, but the steering links exist without any joint that moves them.
